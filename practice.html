Speed Ball

A timed, fast-paced game for the last 15 to 25 minutes of practice, best for ages 8 and up. Every player gets lots of at-bats and fielding chances, runs the bases live, and reacts to real game situations, all while having fun.

Setup: Split the team into 3 groups of 3 to 5 players: Hitters, Infield, and Outfield. A coach pitches (or use a tee or soft toss for younger players). A coach or a net can catch.

How to play:

  1. The hitting group bats in order, and every hitter runs out every ball they put in play.
  2. The infield and outfield groups play live defense, making real plays, throws, and cutoffs.
  3. Hitters who reach base stay on and run the bases, so runners have to read the ball, tag up, and decide whether to take the extra base.
  4. Each rotation runs on a clock (for example, 5 to 8 minutes) instead of outs. The hitting group tries to score as many runs as possible before time runs out, and outs just send the next hitter up. To keep it moving, cap each at-bat at about 4 pitches.
  5. Rotate right away. Outfield moves to infield, infield moves to hitting, and hitters grab their gloves and go to the outfield. Players should sprint between rotations.
  6. After every group has hit, the group with the most runs wins. Play a second round if there’s time.

Game situations: Before an inning, the coach can set the scene by putting runners on base, calling out the outs, or giving a count (for example, runner on 2nd with 1 out). Players then have to make the right play, such as the cutoff throw, hitting behind the runner, or holding the runner.

Coaching tips: Post each group’s run total so the competition stays alive. Coach between pitches, not between innings, so the pace stays fast. Make sure every player gets turns at different positions.

HittingSuggested ages 6-12

Tee Contact Progression

Setup and equipment

One batting tee, a bucket of balls, a bat, and a net or an open area in front of the hitter. Mark the stance so the tee starts around belt height, over the middle of the plate.

How to run it

  1. Start at the middle location. The hitter takes a calm swing, resets the ball, and meets it out in front.
  2. Move the tee a few inches inside, then a few inches outside. Keep the stance. The swing adjusts to the spot.
  3. Raise the tee a little, then lower it a little, so the hitter matches the height without dropping the hands or standing up.
  4. Take about 8 to 12 swings at each spot, then rotate if players are sharing the tee.

Coaching points

  • Contact is in front of the front hip. Swings that meet the ball deep get under it or miss it.
  • Ask for a balanced finish. If the hitter is falling toward the plate, slow the swing down before you ask for more effort.
  • Say the location before the swing so the hitter has a plan.
HittingSuggested ages 7-14

Soft-Toss Progression

Setup and equipment

A tosser, a hitter, a bucket of balls, a bat, and a net or fence. The tosser stands a few steps in front and off to the side, clear of the swing. No machine is required.

How to run it

  1. Start with easy front toss to the middle of the plate. The hitter tracks the ball into the barrel.
  2. Switch to side toss from the hitter's open side so the ball travels across the plate and the hitter lets it get there.
  3. If the hands are rushing, take a short round from one knee, then stand back up and hit the same toss.
  4. About 10 to 15 swings, then switch jobs.

Coaching points

  • The toss is a target for a good swing. Stop the round if the tosses get wild.
  • Keep the head quiet long enough to see the ball meet the bat.
  • A shorter, on-time swing comes before harder contact.
FieldingSuggested ages 8-14

Short-Hop Fielding

Setup and equipment

Gloves, a bucket of balls, and a flat patch of infield dirt or short grass. A coach stands about 15 to 25 feet from the fielders. A cone marks the starting spot.

How to run it

  1. The coach throws or rolls a ball that bounces once just in front of the fielder.
  2. The fielder starts ready, moves through the hop, and fields it out front with two hands when they can.
  3. After the catch, the fielder sets the feet and makes a short throw back to the coach or to a partner.
  4. Each player takes 6 to 8 hops, then the next player steps in.

Coaching points

  • Move through the ball. Stopping and reaching leaves the hop in a hard place.
  • Let the glove give as the ball arrives. A stiff stab pops the ball out.
  • Watch the hop into the glove, then find the target for the throw.
FieldingSuggested ages 9-14

Relay and Cutoff

Setup and equipment

Gloves and balls. One player in the outfield, one at a cutoff spot in the infield, and a coach or catcher at a base. The coach hits or rolls the ball to the outfielder.

How to run it

  1. The outfielder fields the ball and throws to the cutoff player at a height that player can catch.
  2. The cutoff player lines the body up with the base and relays the throw.
  3. On some reps the coach calls "cut," and the cutoff player holds the ball or redirects it to a different base.
  4. Rotate so everyone fields, cuts, and receives.

Coaching points

  • The cutoff player is the target. Outfielders throw to a person, not past them into the infield.
  • Call the base out loud so the throw has a destination.
  • A throw the next player can catch is the right throw.
ThrowingSuggested ages 7-14

Four-Corner Throwing

Setup and equipment

Four players, one near each base or at four cones set like a diamond. Each player has a glove. Start with one ball.

How to run it

  1. Throw around the diamond in one direction. Point the feet at the target before the ball leaves the hand.
  2. After a clean lap, reverse the direction.
  3. Then skip a base, home to second and first to third, so the throw is longer and the receiver shows a target.
  4. If a throw is offline, pause, fix the feet, and start that lap again. Add a second ball only when the throws are catchable.

Coaching points

  • Point the front shoulder and the glove at the target before the arm comes through.
  • Catch with two hands and step into the next throw. A flat-footed catch leads to a late throw.
  • Shorten the distance if the ball is bouncing there. Accuracy first, then a longer throw.
ThrowingSuggested ages 9-14

Partner Long Toss

Setup and equipment

Pairs with gloves, spread along a foul line or across the outfield so groups are not throwing across each other. Start at a comfortable distance, about 30 to 40 feet.

How to run it

  1. Easy catch to warm up, with a slight arc on the ball.
  2. Each pair takes a step back every few throws until the distance is a stretch they can still catch. Younger players should not chase maximum distance.
  3. Use a crow hop when the distance needs it.
  4. Walk toward each other and finish with a few firmer, accurate throws at a shorter distance.

Coaching points

  • The arm should feel loose. If a player feels sharp pain, they stop and tell the coach.
  • Distance comes from the legs and a long throw, not from pushing the ball with a dropped elbow.
  • Pair players who can catch each other. A ball in the dirt every throw is not the drill.
BaserunningSuggested ages 8-14

Base-Running Reads

Setup and equipment

A base, a runner, and a coach with a bat and a ball. Other players can shag. Start with a runner on first. Move the same idea to second once it looks comfortable.

How to run it

  1. The runner takes a primary lead. The coach shows a pitch, and the runner takes a short secondary lead.
  2. The coach hits or points to a spot: back to the infield on the runner's side, through the infield, or in the air to the outfield.
  3. On a ball through the infield, the runner goes. On a ball hit right at an infielder, the runner gets back or holds. On a ball in the air, the runner finds it before deciding.
  4. Rotate runners every few reps.

Coaching points

  • The read comes off the batted ball, not as a guess before contact.
  • On a fly ball, find it before leaving the base area. Tag up when it is caught.
  • Get back to the bag on a line. A wide loop is an easy tag.
FieldingSuggested ages 7-13

First-Step Ground Balls

Setup and equipment

Gloves, a bucket of balls, and a starting spot marked with a cone. The coach is about 20 to 30 feet away on dirt or short grass.

How to run it

  1. The fielder starts ready, with the knees bent and the glove out front.
  2. The coach rolls a mix to the forehand, the backhand, and right at the fielder, without announcing the direction.
  3. The first step goes toward the ball. The fielder plays it out front and makes a short throw.
  4. About 8 to 10 balls, then the next fielder steps in.

Coaching points

  • The first step goes toward the ball. A hop straight up wastes the play.
  • Get the glove to the ground early and field the ball in front of the body.
  • Stay low through the catch. Standing up early lets the ball skip under the glove.
